# Pilot revision V2 — continuity master prompt

## Objective

Replace the first edit's implied transformation with an explicit, readable cause-and-effect chain:

`broken fan → fan enters bucket → banana enters bucket → three shakes → repaired fan exits bucket → banana propeller spins`

The same compact grey tabletop fan, yellow smiley bucket, Bongo design and warm garage workshop must remain continuous throughout.

## Master prompt

Create a clear, continuous 12-second vertical 9:16 3D-cartoon sequence using the provided Bongo character reference. Preserve Bongo’s exact brown fur, huge ears, asymmetric eyes, vivid blue ring around the smaller right eye, single front tooth, banana peel on his head and exactly one blue sandal on his left foot. Keep the same warm garage workshop, the same yellow smiley bucket and the same small grey tabletop fan throughout.

The story must be visually understandable without dialogue:

### Shot 1 — Obviously broken fan, 0.0–2.5 seconds

Show Bongo standing beside the yellow bucket and a very small tabletop fan that clearly does not work. One normal grey fan blade hangs crooked and loose inside the cage, the fan head droops downward, and the power button produces one weak mechanical click. The blades remain completely motionless. Bongo presses the button once, looks at the broken fan and tilts his head. He says: “Bongo… no no no.”

### Shot 2 — Fan goes into the bucket, 2.5–5.0 seconds

The exact same small broken fan is now beside the exact same yellow bucket. Bongo lifts the complete fan with both hands and visibly lowers it all the way into the open bucket. The fan must clearly disappear inside the bucket; do not use a cut or hide the action. Immediately afterward, Bongo drops one intact yellow banana into the same bucket. He looks confident and says: “Bucketo.”

### Shot 3 — Repair ritual, 5.0–8.0 seconds

Bongo grips the bucket with both hands. The broken fan and banana are completely hidden inside it. He performs exactly three clearly separated side-to-side shakes: small, medium, strong. The bucket returns to the center after every shake. Synchronize the voice: “Banga… banga… BANGA!” No objects may escape or duplicate.

### Shot 4 — Repaired fan comes out, 8.0–10.0 seconds

Bongo carefully tips the bucket forward. The exact same grey tabletop fan slides out and lands upright on the floor. It is now visibly repaired: the loose grey blades have been replaced by one large, unmistakable banana-shaped propeller attached securely to the fan hub. Show the transformation result clearly before the fan starts moving. Bongo says in delighted wonder: “OOOOOO… perfecto.”

### Shot 5 — Working payoff, 10.0–12.0 seconds

The banana propeller starts spinning rapidly. The fan body stays planted and stable. The airflow gently pushes back the outer edges of Bongo’s ears and fur. Bongo places one hand proudly on his hip, looks at his repaired fan and says dryly and matter-of-factly: “Bongo fixo.” Hold the final image briefly after the line.

## Continuity rules

It must always be unmistakably the same Bongo, the same yellow smiley bucket and the same small grey fan. The fan must be small enough to fit completely inside the bucket. Show the complete chain clearly. Do not skip any transformation step. Do not introduce another fan, another bucket, loose replacement parts, additional characters, text, subtitles, explosions or camera movement. No object duplication, morphing, teleportation or unexplained jump cuts. Use simple hard cuts between shots, a static eye-level camera and one main action per shot. Prioritize visual clarity, object continuity and readable cause and effect over elaborate motion.

## Production route

Generate and quality-check the five shots separately. Use accepted end states as visual references for the next shot where possible. Do not assemble V2 until every shot independently passes its continuity gate.
