# Pilot 002 — Motion Rebuild V2

Updated: 2026-09-29 (Europe/Berlin)

## Decision

`output/pilot_002/pilot_002_final_v1.mp4` is **rejected as a publishable final video**. It remains useful as a story and timing proof because the joke, cause-and-effect chain, final image and voice placement are understandable.

The rejection is not about the idea. The problem is motion: several accepted units were built from a small number of locked states with timed blends. That solved object duplication and continuity errors, but it does not create believable animation. Bongo appears to dissolve between poses, body weight resets across cuts and the episode feels like a moving storyboard instead of one continuous scene.

## What remains locked

- Story: kernel hits tooth → kernel enters bucket → banana follows → three taps → self-rumble → popcorn eruption.
- Immediate tooth-PING hook.
- Same Bongo, workshop and yellow smiley bucket.
- Planted bucket; no return to the failed hand-held shake.
- Approximately 15-second runtime.
- Voice master containing `Bucketo.` and `Bongo snacko.` in one speaker session.
- Final popcorn-mound composition and dry last line.

## What must be rebuilt

- Real in-between motion instead of dissolves between still poses.
- Continuous hand, arm, torso, ear and eye movement through each action.
- Visible anticipation, contact, follow-through and recovery.
- Motion continuity across cuts: the next shot inherits the previous direction and energy.
- A payoff eruption with moving popcorn, Bongo's delayed reaction and a natural settle.
- Sound bridges across cuts so the edit feels like one event rather than four separate tests.

## New production method

1. Use the accepted keyframes only as identity and composition anchors.
2. Generate true motion clips in Google Flow with Veo, not image dissolves.
3. Every source clip gets at least 8–12 frames of motion handle before and after the intended edit point.
4. Cut during compatible movement; do not cut only after every character has become static.
5. Do not use cross-dissolves to simulate body movement. A dissolve is allowed only for a deliberately stylized transition, never for a paw tap, recoil or reaction.
6. Prototype at lower cost, but regenerate the accepted movement at the highest practical motion quality.
7. Review at normal speed, half speed and frame by frame before acceptance.

## Shot plan

### Shot A — tooth-PING hook, about 0.0–3.2 seconds

One continuous close-to-medium motion: Bongo leans toward the kernel, bites, recoils from the tooth impact, ears lag behind, the kernel follows a readable curved path into the bucket and Bongo's eyes track it downward. The camera makes only a tiny motivated follow. No frozen pose dissolve.

Acceptance gates:

- Impact occurs within 0.8 seconds.
- Tooth remains attached.
- One kernel follows one uninterrupted trajectory.
- Torso recoil and delayed ear motion make the hit feel physical.
- Bongo's gaze finishes on the bucket, providing the movement direction for Shot B.

### Shot B — banana decision and drop, about 3.2–6.5 seconds

Begin with Bongo already looking into the bucket. His eyes flick to the banana, the shoulder leads the reach, the paw carries one banana over the rim, releases it and follows through downward. The banana crosses the rim and disappears while Bongo's hand naturally rises into the confident gesture used for `Bucketo.`

Acceptance gates:

- No pose reset at the cut from Shot A.
- One continuous reach-release-follow-through action.
- Exactly one intact ingredient banana.
- No stem fragment, teleportation or hand snap.
- At least 0.7 seconds of living hold after the drop: breathing, tiny ear settle and eye focus instead of a frozen image.

### Shot C — three physical taps and self-rumble, about 6.5–10.2 seconds

Keep the camera and bucket position locked, but animate Bongo physically. Each tap contains anticipation, acceleration, contact, compression and recoil. His torso transfers weight toward the bucket and returns. After the third contact, Bongo freezes briefly while residual ear motion settles; then the bucket rumbles independently and he reacts late.

If one Veo generation cannot produce exactly three credible taps, create three real motion cycles from the same neutral pose and join them on matching moving frames. The result must still play as one continuous ritual with uninterrupted room tone—never as three still-image dissolves.

Acceptance gates:

- Exactly three visible contacts with full separation.
- Bucket never lifts or slides.
- Bongo's paw and torso show weight transfer.
- Clear stillness before the bucket starts moving.
- Reaction begins after, not before, the first self-rumble.

### Shot D — continuous eruption and settle, about 10.2–15.0 seconds

Begin with the bucket's rumble already active. A few pieces pop first, then a large physical fountain follows. Bongo recoils, loses balance and becomes gradually buried. Popcorn falls back under gravity, the mound settles, Bongo resurfaces and lifts one piece. The camera performs one small pullback during the growth and stops before the final line.

Acceptance gates:

- Popcorn originates only inside the bucket.
- Motion visibly accelerates from small pop to broad fountain.
- Bongo reacts to the moving food rather than dissolving into a buried pose.
- The mound settles through real falling motion.
- `Bongo snacko.` receives at least two seconds of calm final screen time.

## Edit and sound rules

- Hard cuts or short invisible match cuts only; no pose-morphing dissolves.
- Preserve continuous room tone across all shots.
- Let the PING, banana plop, three impacts, silent anticipation and eruption overlap the picture cuts where helpful.
- The picture cut is locked before the existing voice master is repositioned.
- Do not call a version final merely because its continuity and technical checks pass.

## Final approval gates

- Motion test: does Bongo visibly travel through each action instead of changing between states?
- Weight test: do feet, torso, paw and bucket obey a consistent sense of mass?
- Flow test: does each cut inherit gaze, direction or energy from the previous shot?
- Still-frame test: are there no double contours or ghosted eyes from blend-based interpolation?
- Normal-speed test: does it feel like a short cartoon rather than an animated storyboard?
- Existing silent-story, voice-identity and technical gates must still pass afterward.
